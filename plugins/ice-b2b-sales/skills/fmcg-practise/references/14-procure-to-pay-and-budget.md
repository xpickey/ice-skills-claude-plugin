# 14 — Procure to pay and budget control (จัดซื้อจัดจ้างถึงจ่ายเงิน และการควบคุมงบประมาณ)

> **Load this when:** sourcing, vendor master, requisition, purchase order, goods receipt, payables, treasury,
> employee advances, petty cash or purchase-budget control are in scope · or when the prospect says "we cannot
> see committed spend until the invoice arrives".
> **Do not load this for:** the trade budget a brand spends *on the retailer* — listing fees, promotion
> budgets, rebate accruals → **10 Trade Spend and Net GP**, a different budget with different owners and
> different postings · stock valuation and costing → **11** · the subcontract and outside-processing purchase
> chain → **12** · how procurement postings land in the ledger → **15** · withholding tax and electronic tax
> documents → **17**.
> **Source basis:** the apparel reference implementation supplies the full module design — purchase
> classification, three receipt flows, the claim cycle, treasury and the budget check points. Where its design
> left a question open, that is recorded here as a discovery question rather than filled in. Every monetary
> figure, rate and threshold in the source was declared an illustration, so none is carried forward.

## 1. The framing — procurement here is several processes sharing one screen

A consumer-goods or branded-apparel business buys things that behave differently in lead time, quality risk and
accounting, then pushes all of them through one purchasing module:

1. **Finished goods bought for resale**
2. **Materials and services sent out for conversion** — printing, embroidery, cut-make-trim (**12**)
3. **General spend, services and fixed assets**

The reference implementation carries this classification through the whole document-numbering spine and **splits
goods receipt into three distinct flows: converted goods (สินค้าแปรสภาพ), stock items, and services, subcontract
and assets.** That is above-average practice, worth carrying forward. **The refinement worth proposing:** there
the purchase class is a *naming convention*, and naming conventions drift the moment a hurried buyer picks the
wrong prefix. Make it a **validated attribute captured at requisition entry**, driving the receipt route, the
account and the approval path — almost free at design time, expensive to retrofit.

## 2. What the module must be able to do

**Supplier master.** Create a vendor from a request package with a system-assigned code · search-before-create
to stop duplicates · assign to one or more operating companies from day one · hold tax registration, addresses,
contacts, bank details and legal name · **set and amend a credit limit per vendor** · activate and deactivate
rather than delete · **attach statutory documents and track their expiry** · group vendors so the payables
ledger splits correctly, and **grade and tag them by process type and product group**.

**Agreements.** Contract agreement per vendor with item list, price and validity dates · **blanket agreement**
with a total committed quantity and a delivery schedule per release round · multi-level approval · issue by
email with print as fallback · reference the agreement from a purchase order so contract pricing applies ·
**warn on expiry and show consumption to date** · a contract register.

**Requisition.** Raise for five object types — inventory item, import item, service, fixed asset with its asset
number, and project — each with its own required fields · capture company, department, location, project,
vendor, estimated price and expected receipt date · type-bearing numbering · **accept a requisition generated by
the sales side through an interface**, so a project sale creates its expense requisition with no re-keying ·
investment-approval form for new projects · sequential multi-level approval with reject-and-return.

**Sourcing and quotation.** Raise a request for quotation and select the vendors invited · issue by email or
print · **let vendors enter prices online**, or key in returned quotations · on-screen price comparison
submitted for approval · force attachment of the quotation document when it arrives manually · keep bid history.

**Purchase order.** **Create only by pulling an approved requisition — direct order entry is deliberately
disabled** · optionally link to a contract so agreed pricing carries · amend location, lines, quantity, price
and delivery date while open · type-bearing numbering · multi-level approval stamping the approver's electronic
signature on the printed form · **email the approved order to the vendor automatically**, print as fallback ·
open and close a purchase-order period · report open orders, history, received quantity by date, outstanding
quantity and **on-time versus late delivery**.

**Receipt.** Receive in **three flows** — converted goods, stock items, and services and assets · accept a goods
receipt **posted by the third-party logistics provider through an interface** with automatic numbering · receive manually at
a company warehouse when goods bypass that provider · **enter estimated landed cost at receipt for imports and
true it up at invoice** · print the goods-received note, and an asset receipt note that feeds fixed assets
(**15**) · raise a **vendor return authorisation** with return request and return note, and receive the vendor's
credit note · **return stock physically at the provider by transferring it back into a company warehouse first** ·
run a **claim and warranty replacement cycle** on dedicated bins — awaiting claim (company) → awaiting claim
(supplier) → replacement into quality-control hold → release to sellable only after inspection passes · capture
a **maintained reason code** on receipt and return, so returns can be analysed rather than merely counted.

**Payable and treasury.** Record a **vendor prepayment or deposit against a purchase order as a percentage of
its value**, separately for goods and for expenses · **net it automatically when the bill is raised** · create
the bill from the purchase order with the receipt upstream, covering imported goods, imported goods **recognised
before the invoice arrives**, domestic goods and general expenses · post actual landed cost onto the import bill
and adjust the earlier estimate · amend an open bill · raise a bill credit from the original bill · route bills
through approval with **a checker step and a hard edit-lock once the checker approves** · create a payment
across selected open bills and print the billing document · pay by cheque or by **bank payment file** · deduct
**withholding tax with more than one service type on a single payment** and produce the electronic certificate
(**17**) · handle foreign-currency payment with rate editing at bill or at payment · **void a payment and
instruct the bank to stop it** · run **petty cash** two ways, as a float replenished by journal and as requester
drawdowns consolidated and uploaded · run **employee advances end to end** with all three settlement cases,
where spend equals, exceeds or falls short of the advance · payables ageing summary and detail · voucher and
vendor bill reporting · multi-currency with a daily automatic rate update.

## 3. The control points that matter

| Control | How it works | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| **No purchase order without an approved requisition** | direct order entry disabled | makes requisition approval binding rather than advisory |
| Sequential multi-level approval on requisition, order, agreement, bill and payment | reject-and-return at each level | the standard segregation-of-duties spine |
| **Checker step with edit-lock after approval** | the bill cannot be changed once the checker signs | closes the classic payables fraud path |
| Purchase-order period open and close | stale commitments cut off | keeps committed spend honest |
| Quality-control hold on claim replacements | release only after inspection passes | keeps unsellable stock out of the availability number every channel reads |
| Statutory document expiry tracking on vendors | alert on expiry | cheap to build, expensive to omit |

**Three controls were still open in the reference design — probe each in a new engagement.** **Matching
tolerance is undefined**: no price or quantity tolerance and no exception route for a partial or over-receipt is
written down, so in a business that routinely receives short it surfaces first in user acceptance testing, the
worst possible moment. **The requisition-to-order price ceiling was pending**: if a buyer can exceed the
approved requisition price at order entry, requisition approval means nothing. **Payables cannot be split by
purchase type natively**: vendor grouping alone is fragile, because the same legal vendor sells you fabric and a
machine, and that deserves a recorded decision.

## 4. Master data and structure

- **Vendor grouping drives the payables ledger split** — trade, other, asset — and is fragile the moment a
  vendor spans categories, which in this sector is normal rather than exceptional.
- **Vendor grading and capability tagging by process type and product group** is what makes sourcing repeatable
  across seasonal drops. Most implementations bolt it on late; designing it in is nearly free.
- **Type-bearing document numbering** across requisition, order, receipt and bill, carrying the purchase class.
- **Quarantine and claim bin topology** — awaiting claim (company), awaiting claim (supplier), quality-control
  hold — inside the location structure defined in **11**.

## 5. What a consumer-goods brand needs here specifically

Name these explicitly in any pursuit — they separate a category-aware proposal from a generic one. **Outside
processing as a first-class purchase type**, since a brand that owns design but not its factories lives on
subcontracting (**12**) · **vendor capability tagging**, so sourcing is repeatable season after season ·
**landed cost estimated at receipt and trued up at invoice**, because duty and freight often exceed the margin
difference between channels and arrive weeks after the goods · **a quarantine topology for claims**, with
inspection as the release gate back to sellable · **deposits with automatic netting**, since overseas suppliers
demand them and manual netting is how deposits get paid twice · **purchasing joined to the third-party logistics provider
by interface, not email**, so on-hand is right on the day of receipt.

## 6. Weak spots to price explicitly

- **Treasury is usually the thinnest area.** Bank reconciliation, cheque clearing, the cheque register and
  payment splitting by withholding-tax treatment all sat at "pending solution", and in Thailand these are
  month-end blockers. Scope treasury as its own workstream rather than assuming it lands free with payables.
- **Vendor performance was a custom report, not a designed capability.** On-time delivery and postponement
  counts drive sourcing in a seasonal business, where a slip of a fortnight kills a drop. Designing it in, with
  a delay-cause code captured at receipt, is worth more than the report.
- **The accounting design may be absent from the process documents** — no journal postings appeared in any
  procurement flow. **Treat a flow with no journal entries as an incomplete blueprint**; price the accounting
  mapping as its own workshop (**15**).

## 7. Budget control — the purchase budget

This is the **internal spending budget** — departments, projects, capital items. It is not the trade budget
spent on the retailer, which lives in **10**, is owned by commercial rather than finance, and posts differently;
a proposal that conflates the two reads as not understanding the business.

> **The one decision that matters: where in the purchase chain is the budget consumed?**

The reference implementation checks at **requisition, purchase order, receipt, invoice, credit note and
journal** — so the budget is committed **when the buyer commits**, not when the invoice lands months later. For
a seasonal buyer that is the difference between a budget that prevents overspend and one that reports it after
the season has ended. **Any competing design that checks only at invoice should be challenged on this point.**

**What the module must do:** maintain a budget by period and by dimension · create and amend a budget through an
approval route · run a fund check at every controlled document type · report budget versus actual · handle the
approval and rejection cycle on amendment.

### Three things to insist on as standard

1. **A three-state view — commitment, obligation, actual.** A seasonal buyer needs *promised*,
   *delivered-but-unbilled* and *paid* as separate numbers. Recorded as a **gap** in the reference
   implementation, and the most-requested budget report in any product-buying business; assume it in scope
   rather than discover it.
2. **Multi-year carry-forward.** Store fit-outs, campaigns and product development straddle year-end. Carrying
   the unspent balance forward with opening and remaining both visible was also a **gap**. Flag it early — it
   touches the fiscal calendar and the project dimension, not just a report.
3. **A defined over-budget behaviour, by document type.** Best practice is not "block everything" — it is **warn
   at requisition, block at purchase order, allow at invoice with a recorded override**.

**What the source left open, and is therefore a discovery question:** the reference documents say the system
shows an error and returns the user to amend the budget, but **do not say whether the check is a hard stop or
can be overridden**, do not finalise the **control level** — department, project or account — and contain **no
budget transfer between units or projects at all**, only amendment. Ask all three directly.

## 9. Challenges, gaps and improvement areas

| What commonly goes wrong | What the reference implementation did | What a better design looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Purchase class carried as a naming convention** | prefixes on document numbers, applied by the buyer | a validated field captured at requisition, driving route, account and approval |
| **Matching tolerance never defined** | bill built from order and receipt, no tolerance or exception route written down | agree price and quantity tolerance and the short-receipt route during design, not in testing |
| **Payables split by vendor grouping alone** | trade, other and asset driven by vendor group | split by purchase type; record the decision rather than working around it |
| **Treasury, vendor performance and journal postings treated as leftovers** | all three pending or delivered as after-the-fact reports | price each as its own scope line — see section 6 |
| **Budget checked only at invoice** | checked at six document types, which is the stronger design | keep the six-point check; challenge any competing design that checks late |
| **Commitment, obligation, actual and carry-forward missing** | both recorded as gaps | assume both in scope from the first estimate |
| **Purchase budget confused with trade budget** | — | keep them separate — different owner, different postings, different file (**10**) |

## 10. Discovery questions

1. What are the three or four different things you buy, and do they follow the same approval path today? ⚑
2. Can a buyer raise a purchase order without an approved requisition — and can the order price exceed the approved requisition price? ⚑
3. When you receive short, what happens to the invoice match, and who decides?
4. For imports, when do you know the true landed cost, and what do you carry until then? ⚑
5. When goods are defective, where do they sit while the claim runs, and what releases them back to sellable?
6. Does your third-party logistics provider post receipts into your system, or does someone re-key them? ⚑
7. At what point is your budget used up — when someone asks, when you order, or when you pay? ⚑ *changes the estimate materially*
8. If someone is over budget, does the system stop them or warn them, who can override, and does unspent budget carry into next year?
9. Do you deduct withholding tax with more than one service type on a single payment, and are advances and petty cash expected in the system? ⚑
10. Can you show me a process document with its journal postings on it? ⚑ *if none exists, the accounting design is unbuilt*
11. **Do you buy anything under an investment-promotion certificate — imported raw material or machinery?** ⚑ — if yes, two controls move upstream into this module and the landed-cost design changes. See the section below and file **18**

**Scoping signals — raise the estimate when you see:** imports at volume, with landed cost, multi-currency and
goods recognised before the invoice arrives · a claim or warranty cycle with quality-control release ·
withholding tax with multiple service types on one payment · employee advances and petty cash expected in the
system · bank payment files and cheque management · budget with commitment, obligation and actual, or multi-year
carry-forward · vendor performance as a decision input · documentation without postings.

## Duty-exempt purchasing under an investment-promotion certificate

> Triggered by discovery question 11 above.

Where the client holds a promotion certificate, the **purchase order and the goods receipt are where
the duty status originates** — and the source documents are what an audit inspects, not the ledger
entries derived from them. Two controls belong in this module rather than downstream: a **certificate
tag validated before posting** on any duty-exempt purchase, and a **block on tagging a purchase to a
certificate that carries no raw-material entitlement**, because out-of-scope use of duty-exempt
material triggers back duty.

The landed-cost design also changes: exempted duty is not a cost component on that receipt, and it
must not be quietly absorbed into the item cost. Full treatment in file **18**.
